### B2 Test one: water or mud?

- **B2.1** (C2): Clarke's team set out to compare water and sediment as eDNA sources for rapid, practical biodiversity assessment of nearshore benthic environments in the Vestfold Hills, East Antarctica. / water / sediment
- **B2.2** (C4): Clarke's team targeted metazoan (multicellular) organisms by PCR-amplifying part of the mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (COI) gene. / So they went looking for animal DNA. / COI
- **B2.3** (C3): The yardstick Clarke's team set: a high proportion of metazoan reads (for efficiency) and the greatest species richness, whilst reflecting the community composition present at the site (which is not examined here). / high proportion of metazoan reads / greatest species richness / sample type / community at the site
- **B2.4** (C5): In Clarke's study, approximately 40% of reads were assigned to 'Metazoa' for both sediment and water eDNA samples. / Reads assigned to Metazoa (approximately) / % / water / sediment
- **B2.5** (C6): In Clarke's study, at sites where both were collected, similar numbers of taxa were detected in water eDNA samples and the top section of sediment cores (paired t-test, P = 0.67). / water / sediment, top section
- **B2.6** (C9): But in Clarke's study metazoan communities from water and sediment eDNA samples were significantly different (P < 0.001). / water / sediment
- **B2.7** (C7, C8): In Clarke's study, almost half of all metazoan species were detected in both sediment and water eDNA samples (47/99, 47.5%). 22 and 30 species were detected exclusively with sediment or water eDNA, respectively. / both: 47 / water only: 30 / sediment only: 22

### B3 Test two: one voyage, two filters, one plankton recorder
