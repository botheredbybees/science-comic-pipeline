# Plan: Does eDNA agree with itself? (a topic comic)

**Question.** When eDNA gives an answer about marine life, how far can we trust it: does it agree with itself?
**Audience.** Marine-science students and curious non-specialists who know that DNA left in seawater can be read, and want to know how far that answer holds.
**Form.** A topic comic: `meta.sources` lists three papers, every claim has a `source.ref`. 40 claims (13 Clarke, 12 Nester, 15 Blanco-Bercial), 37 panels, 8 pages. Papers not used: Suter et al. 2021 (not open access).

## Spine (And / But / Therefore), with whose claim each is

- **And.** eDNA is used to list what lives in a place, and using it for monitoring will need documented evidence of accuracy, reliability and reproducibility (Blanco-Bercial C26; the plain-words definition is Nester C14; Clarke C1, C2).
- **But.** Agreement cannot be assumed: two earlier studies found less than 10% overlap between sediment and benthic water OTUs (Clarke C1). Each paper changes one thing and checks whether the answer holds. Water and sediment: similar numbers of taxa, different communities (Clarke C6, C9). Two filters: 51 species shared, but higher mean richness with LargeVF (Nester C19, C20). Ten laboratories: clusters by ocean region once contaminated samples were removed, but several samples did not cluster and agreement fell from orders to species (Blanco-Bercial C33, C34, C35, C37).
- **Therefore.** The comic puts the two papers that disagree in kind side by side (B5) and does not settle it. It ends on what the authors say is open: how to know a detected organism is really there (Clarke C12), pairing eDNA with other methods (Clarke C13, Nester C24), more tests of volumes and pore sizes (Nester C25), the effect of each protocol step (Blanco-Bercial C39) and species-level results (C40).

## New assets used, and why

- **Split screen**: B2.1 `side` (water, sediment), B3.2 `side` (LargeVF, SmallVF), B5.1 `slope` (two answers with some friction between them).
- **Black box** with a dashed arrow and question mark: B2.3 (the community at the site, "not examined here") and B4.11 (the impact of each protocol step, which the authors say could not be concluded).
- **Bridge**: B6.2 (eDNA and other methods, which two author teams each ask for).
- Not used: the circle of chairs, the stage and the brain (nothing in the claims supports them).

## Where the comic is the comic's own, not a paper's

- B1.4 ("Three studies, three tests: change one thing, then ask whether the answer holds.") is the comic's framing. "Agrees with itself" is the comic's question, not a result any paper reports.
- B5.1 puts Clarke's and Nester's findings side by side: they are different comparisons (water against sediment; two filter set-ups), so the comic does not call them a contradiction.
- Ines's lines are restatements; Otto's lines are questions or jokes. The two jokes (B4.2, B4.9) are flagged `keeps_claims_intact: false` for a human.
